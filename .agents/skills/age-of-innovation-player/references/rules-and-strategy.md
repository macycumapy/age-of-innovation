# Rules and strategy

The current engine and live legal options take precedence over these notes and remembered board-game rules. Source paths are relative to the repository root.

## Economy and setup

Setup bundles homeland, faction, and round bonus. Evaluate the combination against terrain access, expansion routes, income, competencies/palaces, and upcoming round goals. Faction starts differ: Monks begin with a university, for example.

Compare the first expansion sequence as well as the starting buildings: how many additional workshops can the bundle afford, which homeland or one-spade hexes they use, and what tools remain after the first upgrade. A discounted guild beside an opponent is useful, but a starting site with no affordable continuation can turn an apparently strong palace or school opening into repeated funding turns and early passes.

Coins and tools fund construction; scholars support knowledge/development; books have discipline-specific uses. Preserve the colors needed for a planned purchase before converting books to coins.

Only power in bowl III is spendable. Distinguish charging existing tokens from gaining new ones. Sacrifice reduces future capacity; justify it by an affordable immediate opportunity. Offered power may cost points: compare the actual charge with access to useful shared actions.

An accepted offer costs max(0, actual charge − 1) points. Separate movement from bowl I to II from power that reaches bowl III. For a paid offer, name the useful action it enables and count further charging or sacrifice needed; repeated offers can consume a substantial point lead even when each looks affordable. Free charging and paid charging need different evaluations.

Power actions cost 3 for a bridge/scholar, 4 for tools/coins/one spade, 6 for two spades before modifiers. Illusionists pay one less. Ordinary conversions differ: a scholar costs 5 power, tool 3, coin 1, selected book 5 in the current finder. Use live options for quantities and eligibility.

Sources: `app/Domain/GameEngine/Economy/Enums/PowerAction.php`, `app/Domain/GameEngine/Economy/Services/ResourceConversionOptionFinder.php`, `app/Domain/GameEngine/Economy/Services/PlayerIncomeCalculator.php`.

Development rewards are level-specific. For non-blue players, shipping levels 1/2/3 award 2 points / 2 selected books / 4 points; blue players instead receive no reward / 3 points / 2 selected books. Terraforming levels 1/2 award 2 selected books / 6 points. Add conditional round scoring separately. Verify the exact next reward and a use for any books before buying development late in the game.

Sources: `app/Domain/GameEngine/Research/Actions/AdvanceDevelopmentTrackAction.php`, `app/Domain/GameEngine/Economy/Actions/ApplyPowerOfferDecisionAction.php`.

## Buildings, towns, and networks

Upgrade tree: workshop → guild → palace or school; school → university. Workshop-to-guild base cost is two tools and six coins, or three coins beside an opponent. Compare net income after replacing a building, not both buildings' incomes added together.

Check building supply before budgeting an upgrade chain: each player has nine workshops, four guilds, three schools, one university, and one palace. An existing university rules out planning another ordinary university upgrade, even with sufficient resources.

Building power: workshop 1; guild/school/tower 2; palace/university 3; monument 4. Normally a town needs four connected buildings and power seven. A university reduces the building count to three; a monument to two. Annexes reduce the required count and add power. palace_08 reduces required power to six. In the current finder, reaching any existing town hex through the owned adjacency/bridge component rejects the entire new town: existing town hexes are not simply excluded from its building count. Verify that an independent town site has no such connection before funding it.

A connected university and guild with one annex each have power seven and satisfy the reduced building count: 3 + 2 + 1 + 1 power, with the university's three-building minimum reduced by two annexes. Compare this compact town with adding a school or terraforming new hexes. An ordinary university upgrade grants a competency, so its own reward can supply competency_06's two annexes; include placement turns and availability in the budget.

Town connectivity differs from final-network connectivity. Ordinary towns use direct adjacency and owned bridges; exceptions such as palace_14 are explicit. Final networks use their own calculator including shipping. Do not assume shipping connects a town, or guess adjacency from coordinate strings.

For the final network, Moles also connect owned buildings two hexes apart when they share an intermediate board hex; this does not require a stored tunnel edge. The calculator uses the player's shipping level, not the river_workshop bonus's temporary expansion range. Count each connected component using these rules and compare it with opponents and the neutral territory score before valuing another building or shipping step.

Mole paid terraforming through a tunnel costs one additional tool and awards 2 + player count points (four in a two-player game). Compare the offered normal and tunnel options even when both reach the same target. Add the full terraforming and workshop costs; a tunnel reward alone does not make the follow-up build affordable.

Multiple spades can prepare multiple buildable hexes, but the terraforming follow-up permits only one workshop. Budget a separate later action for another prepared hex and verify that it remains reachable.

Protect routes opponents can block. Check the independence of a second town before merging components into an existing town. Balance separate town opportunities against final network ranking.

Before acquiring a neutral building from an innovation or competency, check its actual placement options. Ordinary neutral placement can offer reachable, empty land with another homeland terrain; placing there pays `spadesTo(player.homeland) × max(1, 3 − terraformingLevel)` tools and changes the terrain. A free building does not make that terrain conversion free, and the placement action's parameters may omit the tool cost. Compare a zero-terraform homeland site with the full cost and continuation of other sites. The acquisition does not guarantee a placement: the follow-up may offer only a null target. Starting placements can use a separate workflow; check its rules independently.

## Scoring

When the current tile rewards that goal, construction awards workshop 2, guild 3, school 4, palace/university 5 points. Other tile goals award spade use 2, knowledge step 1, town tile 5, development step 3, innovation 5. These are conditional round awards, not permanent rewards for those actions. Owned abilities can add rewards.

Round tiles also define science bonuses by discipline and level interval. Check payout thresholds and reward timing. Blessed use an effective knowledge level three higher for science bonuses. The additional sixth-round building goal is separate from final rankings.

Final network ranking awards 18/12/6; each knowledge discipline awards 8/4/2. Ties divide occupied-place awards, rounded down. Level-zero players are excluded from knowledge ranking. Two-player games can include neutral knowledge and a configured neutral territory score that occupies ranking places. Compare the entire field.

At the final pass, tools, scholars, books (including unassigned books), and bowl III power each convert to one coin; each pair of bowl II tokens adds one more coin. Add existing coins and award floor(total / 5) points, retaining the coin remainder. Bowl I does not contribute. Compare actions against this automatic conversion and its five-coin thresholds: leftover resources are not necessarily worthless, and a resource exchange may improve the final payout even without enabling construction.

## Planning

Early: favor income and expansion that fund future scoring. Count remaining income collections and retain an affordable next build. A rich economy without usable terrain can stall.

Middle: turn income into towns, competencies, and innovations while aligning construction with round goals. Shared actions, scarce components, and contested hexes can justify acting before the numerically highest immediate reward. A lower-point town tile may fund another profitable action.

Late: prioritize achievable points, pass rewards, and ranking swings. New income needs a remaining payout and a use. Calculate whether a network link or knowledge step actually overtakes a place.

Before final spending, recount each player's largest connected network from the current hex IDs and layout; do not carry forward an approximate building count. Calculate ties across the entire field. For example, against two connected networks of 12, growing from 11 to 12 changes the network award from 6 to 12; reaching 13 gives 18. Check cheap follow-up sites opened by expansion, including an adjacent homeland hex requiring only a workshop. Compare the complete expansion chain with guild round points, scholar rewards, and the exact final resource payout. Once opponents have passed, expansion beyond a secured first place needs another concrete reward.

Shared book and power actions are contested funding and scoring opportunities. Include when each must be taken in a multi-turn budget, and compare taking a smaller payout now with waiting for a larger one that an opponent can remove. If the action is taken, rebuild the remaining budget and scoring comparison before making conversions; do not treat the previously announced payout as secured.

Before declaring an opponent unable to build or a final ranking secured, check their complete funding chain from the visible state. Include quantity exchanges, bowl-II sacrifice, scholars or power converted to tools, books converted to coins, unused spade actions, and prepared terrain. For example, 3 bowl-III power, 2 bowl-II tokens, a scholar, and 2 books can fund a one-spade workshop on prepared terrain: sacrifice once for the 4-power spade action, exchange the scholar for its building tool, and exchange the books for its 2 coins. Check reach, building supply, shared-action availability, and action order as well as resources. This can turn a 12-to-11 network lead into a 12-to-12 tie, changing the awards from 18/12 to 15/15. After opponents pass, recount their final networks and retain resources when further building no longer changes a ranking or another reward.

Before passing, inspect unused special actions, reachable round goals, all owned pass bonuses, and available replacement round bonuses. Do not spend merely because a conversion is legal or delay passing with an unproductive action.

`StrategicPriorities::settlementValue()` scales a supplied settlement heuristic to 40% in rounds 1–2, 70% in round 3, and 100% later, except setup and town-scoring rounds. This is neither a point payout nor a universal prohibition on early towns.

Source: `app/Domain/Automation/Evaluation/Services/StrategicPriorities.php`.

## Strategic decision checks

Use these checks as planning aids, not as a fixed strategy. Verify mechanical details against the current player-visible state and legal options.

- **Check usable knowledge advancement before buying it.** Track the actual cap and unlock status of each discipline, the requirements for unlocking further advancement, and the attainable steps of a multi-step reward. Do not value a blocked step as points, income, or ranking progress. If advancement falls short of the estimate, correct the plan before repeating it; do not keep acquiring scholars without a useful outlet.
- **Check supply and reward order before committing.** Verify the whole upgrade chain against remaining building pieces. Immediate rewards such as science score the schools present at acquisition, including owned neutral schools in the current calculator: compare buying the innovation before upgrading a school away. Four owned schools therefore yield 20 points even though the ordinary school-piece limit is three. Match book colors and total payment to the live innovation options rather than treating books as interchangeable. Source: `app/Domain/GameEngine/Research/Services/InnovationVictoryPointCalculator.php`.
- **Plan town geometry during setup and early expansion.** Name candidate hexes, required buildings, power, bridges or annexes, and the remaining construction costs. Shipping access is not town connectivity. Check a second town's independence before connecting it to an existing town. Compare the timing and reward of a town with the income and round scoring sacrificed to obtain it; do not postpone all town planning until the middle game.
- **Treat an unsecured expansion hex as contested.** Before delaying its occupation for an upgrade or resource action, check the opponent's reach, available spades, and homeland conversion cost. Count the replacement route if that hex is taken. A cheap homeland hex can be worth securing before an income upgrade when losing it makes the planned town substantially more expensive.
- **Give expansion around an existing town its actual purpose.** A workshop beside a founded town can earn round points, income, or improve the final network; do not count it as progress toward another town without verifying eligible, unclaimed town hexes. Compare its full terrain/building cost and remaining income with an affordable expansion at an independent town site. Establish a second site's geometry and funding early enough to finish it, rather than treating every new workshop as interchangeable growth. Expanding the existing group remains reasonable when its concrete payoff is better.
- **Justify a palace inside a founded town independently of town formation.** Extra building power there does not by itself earn another town reward. Compare keeping the guild and funding another town with the palace's immediate points, ability uses, remaining income, pass rewards, and replacement of guild income. Include the tools, coins, conversions, and lost construction opportunities in both lines. A ten-point palace reward alone is not a complete comparison; a palace can still be best when the whole package wins or another town is infeasible.
- **Budget the economy and early tempo after upgrades.** An upgraded workshop no longer provides its previous income. Before an early school or other expensive upgrade, name the next useful construction and budget its terrain, tools, coins, and any funding actions after paying for the upgrade. Compare remaining income collections with the expansion and round points forgone if the upgrade forces an early pass. Repeated short rounds caused by resource exhaustion call for reassessing upgrade timing and income balance. An early school remains reasonable when its full reward funds a useful continuation.
- **Price the long-term cost of sacrificing power.** Check remaining token capacity and whether future charging can still fund useful actions. Heavy early sacrifice needs a concrete payoff; access to an action alone is not enough reason to take it.
- **Compare complete scoring packages.** Include town rewards, unlocks, owned pass bonuses, innovation scaling, and final rankings without double-counting. For example, league_of_cities scales with town tiles; assess its timing against realistic additional towns and competing innovations, not just its current payout. Count actual connected network size rather than all owned buildings.
- **Forecast every player's total at round transitions.** Separate current points from remaining pass rewards, attainable towns/innovations, resource conversion, and network/knowledge rankings. For each player's repeatable pass rewards, calculate the current payout times remaining eligible passes and affordable changes to that payout: include town count, weakest knowledge level, and relevant building counts. Also price repeatable construction rewards: palace_13 and build_guild together award 6 points per guild even outside a guild-scoring round. Include affordable upgrades and the towns those upgrades complete; a sixth-round town can award its tile points, 5 round points, and increased pass rewards together. Use ranges for uncertain continuations and update after a material acquisition. Do not treat a temporary point lead as a projected win. If the forecast shows a deficit, compare feasible lines that can close it and revise the plan; securing an already-led discipline needs a concrete benefit relative to those alternatives. Report a projected deficit candidly rather than describing a single successful action as keeping pace.
- **Price knowledge competition by its marginal return.** Count all scholars, books, power, and turns needed for a ranking change, including attainable caps and opponents' affordable replies. Compare the resulting change in final awards, round points, science bonuses, and unlocked actions with a funded construction or innovation sequence using those resources. An extra town, building-count innovation, and repeatable pass rewards can outweigh winning two knowledge disciplines. Avoid repeatedly reinforcing a lead whose projected benefit is smaller than the expansion or scoring opportunity forgone.
- **Value a competency together with its board-position reward.** Its ability description alone does not include its knowledge/book reward. In the current engine, use its zero-based index in setupPool.competencies, not its index in the shrinking availableCompetencyIds supply: column selects the discipline, and rows 0/1/2 grant 3/2/1 steps plus 0/1/2 books respectively. Taking a later supply copy does not itself change that reward. Add those immediate rewards, remaining income collections or passes, and the actions they fund. For a weakest-knowledge pass ability, budget raising every minimum discipline; for scholar points, count actual remaining scholars and usable destinations. Check the confirmed result before relying on the expected reward. Source: `app/Domain/GameEngine/Research/Actions/GrantCompetencyAction.php`.
- **Budget the final sequence before spending scholars.** List the remaining constructions, terrain work, conversions, and development rewards with their full costs. Compare the affordable sequence against passing now, including automatic resource points. A development step that gives books needs a concrete scoring use for them; greater shipping range needs a verified ranking change if that is its justification.
- **Time repeatable rewards and contested knowledge steps.** Value a pass ability by its current payout times remaining passes and affordable changes to that payout. For knowledge, count the turns needed to reach a ranking threshold and the opponent's available responses. Level 12 is exclusive among players; crossing 7 requires an unused town key. Do not spend steps beyond the attainable cap or chase a rank that no longer changes.
- **Compare alternatives before expensive commitments.** For a major upgrade, innovation, or resource conversion, consider a feasible competing line and the next few actions each enables. Distinguish verified costs/effects from estimates. After confirmation, check actual resource, income, knowledge, and connectivity changes instead of reporting the intended result as accomplished.
- **Price preparation and timing before extending a town plan.** Include bridge, terrain work, funding actions, and placement turns in the town's cost. Compare finishing now with waiting for a town-scoring round: count the income, pass rewards, contested rewards, and blocked building pieces lost while waiting. Two towns are not automatically a successful scoring plan when their preparation consumes the actions that could have earned more elsewhere. Reassess the remaining sequence after a shared action is taken, a reward changes, or an opponent raises the ranking target; previously spent resources do not justify completing a now-inferior line.
- **Verify the destination before irreversible funding conversions.** For a proposed tunnel/build or upgrade sequence, identify the exact offered target, access mode, and full follow-up cost before exchanging scholars, books, or power. A normal route to one hex does not establish that a tunnel option to it exists. If future options cannot yet be verified, keep flexible resources where practical and describe the continuation as conditional. Report a blocked or unaffordable plan promptly instead of presenting successive substitutions as execution of the original plan.
